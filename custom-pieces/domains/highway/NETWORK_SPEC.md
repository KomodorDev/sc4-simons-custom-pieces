## 1. Implementation status

The registered `scp_highway` provider builds a standalone native diagonal DD RHW-4/Avenue crossing the thirteen reusable types for the [compact diagonal diamond](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/README.md), the [modular reference and alternatives](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-modular-diamond/README.md), and the fifteen new types for the [tight parallel diamond](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-parallel-diamond/README.md). A **DAT** is a SimCity 4 resource archive. A **PATH** resource stores directed traffic routes, and an **S3D** resource stores an indexed visible model. Car transport is engine class 1, and pedestrian transport is class 2. Heights are metres above the pavement datum at ground 0 m; no HSR 0.5 m datum is imported.

Implementation entry points are `implementation/scp_highway/native.py` for measured native channel composition, `recipes.py` for resource packaging and explicit construction, `ramp_geometry.py` for authored surfaces and native barrier openings, `slip_lanes.py` for flat auxiliary lanes and shoulder markings, `ramp_paths.py` for exact tile cuts and the coordinated module partition, `ramp_materials.py` for measured native pavement, barrier, and concrete materials, `wheeltracks.py` for the bounded highway-to-ramp wear field, `wheeltrack_verify.py` for independent saved-field and native-colour checks, `verify.py` for the independent native crossing contract, `ramp_verify.py` for saved pavement and corridor checks, `connections.py` for shared intrinsic extraction, and `menu.py` for the Highways-menu donor. The domain registry explicitly selects this provider. New maintained subjects use JSONC definitions; generated machine evidence remains strict JSON.

The earlier `design.jsonc` remains a reproducible schematic source with its original reports. The actual game modules use measured native lane boundaries and grid-snapped geometry. A schematic test or an actual-resource test cannot establish a human game pass.

The compiled experiment chooses the upper deck towards northeast and the lower deck towards southwest. In the native `57530200` orientation, the upper paths instead run from east to south and the lower paths reverse them. The provider deliberately selects and transforms the matching crossing variants, including their complete paired-cell geometry and routes.

**Right-hand traffic** means vehicles use the right side relative to their own travel direction. The current assembly puts upper-deck ramps southeast of the highway and lower-deck ramps northwest of it. No left-hand-traffic compatibility has been established.

## 3. Prototype geometry

This chapter describes the retained historical schematic, before the native modules, the ramp extension, and the right-turn-only revision. Its dimensions and movements are not the current compiled design. The maintained prototype source is [design.jsonc](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/design.jsonc). Its `geometry` object contains all provisional dimensions. Highway deck heights of 7.5 m and 15 m are native-derived; a 12 m deck width, a 24 m avenue width, and a 6 m ramp width are design assumptions. At the user's request, the compact prototype replaces the former 8% grade assumption with a 40% experimental bound, uses 48 m lower ramps, and keeps the higher ramps longer at 80 m. These dimensions and the grade bound must not be presented as measured NAM profiles or engineering standards. The avenue and highway run on perpendicular diagonal axes, at 45° to the city grid.

A **grade** is height change divided by horizontal travel distance; 30% means a rise of 30 m over a horizontal distance of 100 m. The prototype keeps an upper ramp at full deck height until its entire provisional roadway width has moved outside the lower-deck envelope, then follows a steep height transition. This avoids the obvious error of descending through the lower highway. Peak sampled grades are approximately 35.96% for upper ramps and 32.50% for lower ramps; they exceed the whole-ramp averages because the joining sections are flat. Sampled centre-line checks do not establish clearance for full vehicle bodies, barriers, supports, or native S3D geometry.

In that earlier schematic, the four ramps meet the avenue at two surface intersections. Each has two avenue legs, one incoming off-ramp leg, and one outgoing on-ramp leg. These are four-arm intersections, rather than T junctions. Both avenue directions can reach either highway direction; both highway directions can leave towards either avenue direction. Ground intersections introduce intentional conflict points and need a future traffic-control design.

The [geometry generator](tools/diagonal_diamond_geometry.py), [independent prototype verifier](tools/verify_diagonal_diamond.py), and [schematic renderer](tools/render_diagonal_diamond.py) own these experimental calculations. Their output is not a native connection contract, a compiled-model preview, or a saved-resource verification report. These source-only checks do not establish support geometry or final module cuts; the compiled partition is recorded separately below.

## 7. Highway and ramp barrier joints

The compact diamond uses the measured native barrier cross-section: insets and heights `(0, 0)`, `(0.21875, 0.328125)`, `(0.265625, 1.390625)`, `(0.5625, 1.390625)`, `(0.625, 0.328125)`, and `(0.828125, 0)` in metres. An **inset** is distance inward from the outer edge of the road. The native barrier occupies the band from absolute highway-axis lateral position 7.171875 m to 8 m. Clipping every ramp face at 8 m removed part of the joining barrier profile, leaving an inner triangular gap and an outer notch.

`ramp_geometry.py` keeps pavement and deck skirts clipped at the original 8 m boundary. Each barrier-profile vertex instead meets its matching native line at absolute lateral position `8 m − inset`. Short concrete bridge strips connect that intersection to the existing native cut. This joins the two barrier sides and the crown without adding a wall across the driving opening. The **crown** is the barrier's top surface. The repair also gives both swept barrier sides outward-facing triangle order; a two-sided software preview alone cannot establish correct face orientation in the game.

`barrier_mouth_verify.py` independently derives the reference sections from the retained continuous ramp curves and literal native cross-section measurements, without importing the geometry writer. Complete assembly verification checks continuous coverage and outward orientation across 160 barrier-joint sections in each of four rotations, for 640 section checks. Its 3 mm endpoint guard covers the measured difference between analytic and sampled curve anchors. These bounded checks do not establish that every model is a closed solid or that the repaired barriers have been observed in the game.

## 8. Slip-lane marking geometry

A **gore** is the painted triangular area separating the highway through lane from the ramp. Its highway-side boundary follows the straight native lane edge; its ramp-side boundary follows the departing ramp edge. The old implementation used 0.12 m dashes, 0.10 m solid lines, and hatch strokes whose angle changed with the triangle's width. It also ended the gore using an arbitrary offset from a route point. These were authored marking defects, rather than measurements of the NAM ramp artwork.

The corrected authored white markings use a 0.25 m physical bright core for dashes, solid edges, gore outlines, and hatch strokes. This matches the two bright-core texture columns measured in both native DD RHW-4 texture `5713000E` and native MIS texture `5712000E`, at the retained 16 m texture scale; the [native marking measurements](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/evidence/native-marking-measurements.json) retain the input fingerprints, numerical comparison, and saved baseline handoffs. The two bright-core columns match byte for byte; neighbouring antialias pixels do not all match. The retained narrow white texture crop supplies paint without adding an asphalt rectangle around it. Native filtering, antialiasing, mipmaps, lighting, and game appearance remain separate questions.

The dashed separator and the gore's straight boundary share the native lane edge 4.5 m from the highway axis, avoiding the old lateral step between them. Dashes begin after the accepted lane-change gap, where the widening outer line has separated, so the two paint bands do not overlap at the taper start. Its departing boundary starts at the actual revised ramp-path edge and meets the saved native ramp paint at the original 8 m deck boundary. The outer edge uses a smooth curve with a horizontal starting tangent and the measured saved ramp-paint tangent at its end. A **tangent** is the direction a line or curve follows at a particular point. Only the painted outer parallel section is shortened where necessary to make that connection; the accepted driving paths retain their 12 m/4 m lower and 16 m/8 m upper taper/parallel lengths. All hatches use parallel lines at 45° to the highway axis, with their complete painted faces trimmed inside the gore outlines. ## 9. Native wheel-track transition

A **wheel track** here means the dark worn band already present in NAM's pavement image. The field uses only unmarked asphalt portions of the image, so interpolating its texture coordinates cannot drag an unrelated white or yellow stripe onto the road. A half-ribbon is the narrow four-cornered strip between the route centre and one pavement edge. At a tight curve, its edges can cross into a bow-tie shape. The horizontal native texture coordinate is U, and the longitudinal coordinate is V. ### 10.3 Radius families and smooth curves

Preserve the existing compact geometry as its own measured interface family; do not move it merely to make its radius equal one of these targets.

For a circular 90° reference with centred orthogonal tile-edge ports, `R = 8 + 16n` metres provides convenient phases: start at `(east,north)=(-8,0)` heading east, and end at `(R−8,R)` heading north. Radii 24 m, 40 m, and 56 m produce centreline reference grids of 2 × 2, 3 × 3, and 4 × 4 tiles. Those are geometric examples, not established construction footprints for a finished piece. ### 10.4 Standard joining grades and separate interior limits

**Grade** is vertical change divided by horizontal distance along the route; 10% means 1 m of height change over 10 m of horizontal travel. Standardize new exact endpoint grades as **0%, ±7.8125%, ±15.625%, and ±23.4375%**. Call the three nonzero magnitudes Gentle, Standard, and Compact. Preserve the existing ±22.675579% and ±29.609595% joins as measured existing-interface classes, and use adapters where a new standard is wanted. Uphill and downhill are distinct directed cases; matching outward card grades have opposite signs.

| Proposed endpoint-grade magnitude | Constant-grade run for 7.5 m change | Constant-grade run for 15 m change | Orthogonal flat-to-flat example for 7.5 m change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gentle: 7.8125% | 96 m / 6 tile lengths | 192 m / 12 tile lengths | 112 m / 7 tile lengths |

## 13. Adaptive PATH runtime elevation

## 14. Shadows for independent elevated modules

### 14.1 Failure and repair scope

The parallel diamond's reported shadow defect exposed two separate mechanisms. The earlier generated network shadows projected opaque model triangles vertically onto one 16 × 16 m tile, clipped the result at that tile's edges, and ignored height. That recipe discarded overhanging ramp and deck silhouettes and could not reproduce sunlight displacement. The adaptive crossing's visible elevated props also had `IsGroundModel=false`, which prevents ordinary prop shadow creation. Passing a saved-image comparison against the old recipe did not establish either the game's selected shadow or its physical footprint.

The repair uses each component's own saved, referenced three-dimensional triangles to create a separate invisible **shadow carrier**, meaning an attached model whose texture supplies the shadow while its geometry draws no visible surface. The fifteen parallel types explicitly select `shadow_mode: "model-carrier"`, clear their old `shadow_mappings`, and declare every owned cell in `model_shadows`. Twenty-five cells contain elevated geometry and receive carriers; the six ground-only terminal cells declare `cast_shadow: false`. Each carrier uses only its owner's geometry, including overhang. Placing a single departure must not cast the missing neighbouring highway or the rest of an assembly. Original visible models, traffic PATHs, permanent construction identifiers, and cell ownership remain separate from these shadow resources.

The [frozen native contract](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-parallel-diamond/evidence/model-shadow-native-contract.json) records the fingerprinted Windows SimCity 4 1.1.641.0 executable, measured lighting, instruction addresses, resource contract, and limits. ### 14.2 Selected resources and invisible geometry

Each elevated owner receives four S3D carrier models in group `BADB57F1`, four ordinary FSH mask textures in group `1ABE787D`, and one prop attached by an exact-tile T21. Property `029244DB` is present as a scalar Boolean **false**, and `69F14D33` is false. ### 14.3 Sunlight and the four relative poses

The supported sunlight profile is `sc4-default-67p5-45`: azimuth 67.5° and elevation 45°, selected by lighting exemplar `6534284A-A9189CF0-00000001`. Its decompressed fingerprint is `14e29418b419fd89f4f06f697f8137f4ce933157be9b63d0260a743e19098ced`. The build audits the current lighting dependency and rejects unsupported overrides. This lighting is fixed to the screen; turning the city camera changes its direction in world coordinates. A world-fixed shadow assumption would therefore fail other camera views. Native Float32 storage gives the default sun vector `(0.6532814502716064, -0.7071067690849304, -0.27059805393218994)` and the carrier-local vector `s = (-0.27059805393218994, -0.7071067690849304, -0.6532814502716064)`. The trigonometric reconstruction retains the measured Float32 storage boundaries; it is not a replay of the native x87 instructions.

Let `R1(x,y,z) = (-z,y,x)` be one quarter turn, let `r` be the object's proper rotation, and let `c` be the camera rotation, each numbered 0–3. Native shadow selection chooses variant `j = (r-c-1) mod 4` and applies `R(c+1)` to its carrier. Variant `j` projects `Rj` of the original geometry along `s` onto ground using `(x-y*sx/sy, z-y*sz/sy)`. For a required ground corner `(qx,qz)`, its stored carrier point is `(qx+y*sx/sy, y, qz+y*sz/sy)`, with corner heights `(0,64,64,0)`. Projecting this slanted point returns the intended ground corner; the texture-coordinate mapping remains affine, meaning a single linear mapping plus an offset covers the complete quadrilateral. The resulting transform agrees with the original geometry for all sixteen object/camera rotation combinations. The four variants preserve every selected triangle's actual height and ramp slope before rasterization; they do not replace two decks with one flat silhouette.

The source material guard rejects elevated meshes with alpha testing or blending enabled rather than treating a future transparent cutout as solid. The current native DD, MIS, barrier, and concrete materials use flags `2A`, which disable both mechanisms. The [frozen opacity audit](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-parallel-diamond/evidence/model-shadow-opacity-audit.json) confirms that the audited DD, barrier, and concrete texture candidates are wholly opaque. MIS has transparent outer columns, but the actual ramp U coordinates, 0.3125–0.6875, sample only opaque columns 39–88 under first-image bilinear filtering, inside the texture's opaque columns 16–111; every row has the same alpha. These facts justify the current opaque-triangle silhouette without claiming general support for alpha-cutout materials. Material semantics are documented in the [pinned graphics contract](https://github.com/tloriato/OpenSC4/blob/c6b1887782bfa457dc6211852887339ef5f7cd84/docs/specs/graphics-formats.md#38-mats-compat).
