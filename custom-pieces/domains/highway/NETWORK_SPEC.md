## 1. Implementation status

The registered `scp_highway` provider builds a standalone native diagonal DD RHW-4/Avenue crossing and the thirteen reusable types for the [compact diagonal diamond](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/README.md). A **DAT** is a SimCity 4 resource archive. A **PATH** resource stores directed traffic routes, and an **S3D** resource stores an indexed visible model. Car transport is engine class 1, and pedestrian transport is class 2. Heights are metres above the pavement datum at ground 0 m; no HSR 0.5 m datum is imported.

Implementation entry points are `implementation/scp_highway/native.py` for measured native channel composition, `recipes.py` for resource packaging and explicit construction, `ramp_geometry.py` for authored surfaces and native barrier openings, `ramp_paths.py` for exact tile cuts and the coordinated module partition, `ramp_materials.py` for measured native pavement, barrier, and concrete materials, `verify.py` for the independent native crossing contract, `ramp_verify.py` for saved pavement and corridor checks, `connections.py` for shared intrinsic extraction, and `menu.py` for the Roads-menu donor. The domain registry explicitly selects this provider. New maintained subjects use JSONC definitions; generated machine evidence remains strict JSON.

The earlier `design.jsonc` remains a reproducible schematic source with its original reports. The actual game modules use measured native lane boundaries and grid-snapped geometry. A schematic test or an actual-resource test cannot establish a human game pass.

## 2. Measured native inputs

The initial audit reads the user's installed NAM resources and original game archive without copying their binary payloads into the repository. The portable [native audit](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/evidence/native-audit.json) records archive fingerprints, resource identities, path samples, and coverage limits. These facts describe the audited bytes, rather than all NAM versions or an in-game observation.

| Native input | Measured fact | Scope limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `RealHighway_DD_RHW-4.dat` | The audited archive contains stacked diagonal car paths at 7.5 m and 15 m. | These are PATH heights; they are not measured deck underside heights or available vehicle clearance. |
| Diagonal DD RHW-4 PATH `296678F7-A966883F-57530200` | Four real car routes represent two lanes on each opposing deck; the resource also includes two explicitly labelled invalid capacity routes. | Exclude the invalid capacity paths from actual journey claims. |
| Diagonal crossing PATH pair `5753A300` and `5753A305`, under type/group `296678F7-A966883F` | Native diagonal DD RHW-4 over diagonal avenue content exists. | These are draggable-network resources; no direct RUL0 construction family was found for this crossing. A new selectable construction cannot be obtained merely by renaming its resource IDs. |
| Reverse-flow crossing pair `5753A380` and `5753A385` | The native data also provides the opposite choice of upper/lower travel direction. | Native placement and complete joining boundaries still require independent checks. |
| `SimCity_1.dat`, PATH `296678F7-69668828-04000200` | A diagonal avenue half has two car routes and a pedestrian route at ground height 0 m. | One half does not establish the complete two-tile avenue boundary or its rotated construction. |

The compiled experiment chooses the upper deck towards northeast and the lower deck towards southwest. In the native `57530200` orientation, the upper paths instead run from east to south and the lower paths reverse them. The provider deliberately selects and transforms the matching crossing variants, including their complete paired-cell geometry and routes.

**Right-hand traffic** means vehicles use the right side relative to their own travel direction. The current assembly puts upper-deck ramps southeast of the highway and lower-deck ramps northwest of it. No left-hand-traffic compatibility has been established.

## 3. Prototype geometry

This chapter describes the retained historical schematic, before the native modules, the ramp extension, and the right-turn-only revision. Its dimensions and movements are not the current compiled design. The maintained prototype source is [design.jsonc](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/design.jsonc). Its `geometry` object contains all provisional dimensions. Highway deck heights of 7.5 m and 15 m are native-derived; a 12 m deck width, a 24 m avenue width, and a 6 m ramp width are design assumptions. At the user's request, the compact prototype replaces the former 8% grade assumption with a 40% experimental bound, uses 48 m lower ramps, and keeps the higher ramps longer at 80 m. These dimensions and the grade bound must not be presented as measured NAM profiles or engineering standards. The avenue and highway run on perpendicular diagonal axes, at 45° to the city grid.

A **grade** is height change divided by horizontal travel distance; 30% means a rise of 30 m over a horizontal distance of 100 m. The prototype keeps an upper ramp at full deck height until its entire provisional roadway width has moved outside the lower-deck envelope, then follows a steep height transition. This avoids the obvious error of descending through the lower highway. Peak sampled grades are approximately 35.96% for upper ramps and 32.50% for lower ramps; they exceed the whole-ramp averages because the joining sections are flat. Sampled centre-line checks do not establish clearance for full vehicle bodies, barriers, supports, or native S3D geometry.

In that earlier schematic, the four ramps meet the avenue at two surface intersections. Each has two avenue legs, one incoming off-ramp leg, and one outgoing on-ramp leg. These are four-arm intersections, rather than T junctions. Both avenue directions can reach either highway direction; both highway directions can leave towards either avenue direction. Ground intersections introduce intentional conflict points and need a future traffic-control design.

The [geometry generator](tools/diagonal_diamond_geometry.py), [independent prototype verifier](tools/verify_diagonal_diamond.py), and [schematic renderer](tools/render_diagonal_diamond.py) own these experimental calculations. Their output is not a native connection contract, a compiled-model preview, or a saved-resource verification report. These source-only checks do not establish support geometry or final module cuts; the compiled partition is recorded separately below.

## 4. Compiled geometry and verification

The current compact partition owns 53 construction tiles inside a 10 × 10-tile bounding rectangle (160 × 160 m). Thirteen reusable types supply fifteen placements: a coordinated access core, two surface junctions, four highway branch modules, four graded modules, and two complementary native continuation halves. Continuation A and continuation B are each used twice. The two outer native continuation cells are owned by the upper branches because the extended ramps also have road faces in those cells. Shared native and authored services on one city tile have one owner. Normal builds preserve all selected types; assembly-menu creates one Roads button with Tab/Shift+Tab cycling in a separate build.

Each of the four highway ramp connections has moved one diagonal city-grid step farther from the avenue: 16 m along both city axes, or approximately 22.6274 m along the highway. The complete lower source curve is approximately 69.9880 m: 30.9128 m of level highway joining curve, 35.0752 m of active height transition, and 4 m of level ground landing. The upper source curve is approximately 101.9401 m: 45.2809 m level, 52.6593 m graded, and 4 m level. These are horizontal distances measured along the source curves. Source peak grades are approximately 22.6756% lower and 29.6096% upper. The revised authoring bound is 30%; the saved PATH segments establish the compiled candidate’s actual maximum independently.

The current traffic policy allows four highway or avenue through journeys and four adjacent-carriageway right turns: lower highway from northeast to avenue northwest, avenue from northwest to lower highway southwest, upper highway from southwest to avenue southeast, and avenue from southeast to upper highway northeast. The four corresponding left turns across the opposing avenue carriageway are excluded. Both avenue directions and both avenue pedestrian channels remain continuous. This policy removes the authored cross-avenue car routes and their crossing pavement aprons; Route Query and visible traffic still require separate human observations.

Measured native deck width is 16 m, pavement heights are 7.5 m and 15 m, native vertical edge skirts extend approximately 1 m below the pavement, and native barriers rise approximately 1.390625 m above their deck. Barrier cuts are restricted to the ramp’s actual side and deck; opening a lower-deck ramp leaves the upper barrier intact. The opposite side of each upper-deck branch remains closed. Some native model faces extend approximately 1.657 m beyond a construction tile. Construction ownership and the complete physical envelope are separate measurements. Frozen original triangle/material fingerprints, or strictly contained fragments on those original faces, bound the native-overhang exception; authored ramp faces receive no equivalent exception. Authored ramp surfaces are 6 m wide and are clipped at actual city-tile boundaries. Empty PATH resources are valid only in independently declared model-only shoulder cells; an actual native empty-PATH measurement is retained in the core audit.

Ramp appearance comes from NAM’s single-lane ramp network, MIS, and the retained DD RHW-4 highway. The [native ramp-material audit](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/evidence/native-ramp-materials-audit.json) records the audited MIS slope model `57722000`, its material fingerprints, and the exact referenced texture resources. All authored ramp pavement, including the high joining sections and ground turns, uses native MIS texture `5712000E`. Retained native DD RHW-4 mainline faces keep their original pavement texture `5713000E`. Barriers and concrete use `5EB4BEE9` and `5EB4BEEE`. An **FSH** resource is a saved texture image. These native FSH images stay in the installed NAM texture packs, and the custom models reference them without copying their image bytes. The source replaces the previous flat colour swatches and extra paint strips with native materials and continuous texture coordinates, preserving native marking scale across construction cuts. A texture reference curve is the centre line used to measure pavement distance and width for image placement. The ramp and its ground turn share one continuous reference curve through the mouth, and each pavement face uses one curve consistently. Overlapping mouth fragments have one pavement owner. This appearance mapping does not claim that the custom curve or grade is a native NAM geometry piece.

The horizontal texture coordinate **U** runs from 0 at the image’s left edge to 1 at its right edge. The 3.5 m-wide ground turns use the unmarked native MIS pavement strip at U = 0.375–0.59375, with a smooth transition from the ramp’s edge markings below 1.5 m pavement height. Only flat authored ground-pavement triangles are subdivided into faces with edges at most 1 m long, allowing the texture’s wheel-track pattern to follow the curve. This preserves the original 388.109375 m² of flat ground pavement, its planes, its boundaries, and its tile ownership. For one unrotated (0°) source layout before binary encoding, the [material audit](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/evidence/native-ramp-materials-audit.json) counts 3,432 authored ground-pavement triangles before subdivision and 55,808 afterwards. These source counts exclude native faces, elevated faces, piers, and other rotations. Native model faces, traffic routes, and allocated identifiers remain unchanged.

The independent saved-resource verifier reads actual indexed triangles and every encoded car segment. It checks continuous pavement coverage, permitted grade, exact model-only cells, native material identities and texture coordinates, and triangle-face intersections with a 2.5 m-wide, 3.8 m-high graded corridor in all four proper rotations. This is not full solid containment, a complete swept vehicle-body calculation, a structural proof, a native neighbour compatibility catalogue, or a game observation. The native crossing additionally pins its independently composed model and route measurements. Assembly route reachability must be checked at the registered saved poses, including the four excluded cross-avenue turns.

The native donors have no horizontal underside face under the tile centre. The assembly’s authored 0.8 m-square concrete column surfaces run from ground 0 m through the lower pavement at 7.5 m to the upper pavement at 15 m; no top cap is placed coplanar with the road. The [assembly testing record](01_assemblies/ddrhw-l1-and-l2-diagonal-avenue-diamond/TESTING.md#5-results-and-known-issues) owns the current saved column count, pavement contacts, physical envelope, seam measurements, and executed checks. Their saved-face corridor and visual contact checks are separate from structural engineering.

Junction control and game traffic behaviour, steep-grade vehicle animation, terrain behaviour, shadows, placement, demolition, and save/reload require their respective evidence. Human observations stay unverified until explicitly confirmed. Building, installing, publishing, and merging are separate actions; only the human maintainer merges a pull request.
